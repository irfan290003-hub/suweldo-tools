2. Who Qualifies vs. Who Is Exempt Under Philippine Labor Law

Not all employees are automatically entitled to statutory overtime pay, holiday pay, rest day premium pay, or night shift differential. Under Article 82 of the Labor Code, it is critical to distinguish covered employees from exempt classifications:

Employees Who Qualify for Statutory Benefits

  • Private-Sector Rank-and-File Employees: Covered regardless of whether employment status is regular, probationary, casual, project-based, or fixed-term.
  • Monthly-Paid and Daily-Paid Workers: Both qualify for overtime, rest day, holiday, and night shift premiums, subject to how their daily basic wage is derived.

Statutory Exemptions (Labor Code Art. 82, 86 & 94)

  • Managerial Employees & Managerial Staff: Those vested with powers or prerogatives to lay down and execute management policies or hire, transfer, suspend, or discharge employees.
  • Field Personnel: Non-agricultural employees who regularly perform their duties away from the principal place of business and whose actual hours of work cannot be determined with reasonable certainty.
  • Retail & Service Establishments Size Thresholds: Establishments regularly employing fewer than 10 workers are exempt from statutory Regular Holiday Pay (Art. 94), and those regularly employing not more than 5 workers are exempt from Night Shift Differential (Art. 86).
  • Workers Paid by Results / Piece-Rate & Kasambahay: Governed by specific DOLE piece-rate orders and Republic Act No. 10361 (Batas Kasambahay).

3. Conditions That Affect Holiday Pay Eligibility & Monthly Divisors

A. Attendance on the Workday Immediately Preceding a Regular Holiday

Under the Implementing Rules of Book III of the Labor Code and the DOLE Handbook, a covered employee is entitled to the 100% unworked Regular Holiday pay if they were present or on leave of absence with pay on the workday immediately preceding the regular holiday. If the employee was absent without pay (AWOL / LWOP) on the workday immediately preceding the regular holiday, they may forfeit the unworked holiday pay unless they actually report for work on the regular holiday itself (in which case they are entitled to 200% for the hours worked).

B. Successive Regular Holidays (e.g., Maundy Thursday & Good Friday)

Where there are two (2) successive regular holidays, an employee who is absent without pay on the day immediately preceding the first holiday may not be paid for both holidays—unless the employee works on the first regular holiday, in which case they become entitled to holiday pay on the second regular holiday.

C. Monthly-Paid vs. Daily-Paid Payroll Presentation

For monthly-paid employees whose salary is computed using the 365-day factor, the basic 100% pay for all regular holidays and rest days is already integrated into their fixed monthly salary. Consequently, when a monthly-paid employee works 8 hours on a Regular Holiday, their payslip typically shows their regular monthly basic salary plus an additional +100% holiday premium (bringing total compensation for that day to 200%). Our calculator separates Estimated Regular Pay (100%) from Additional Holiday / Rest Day Pay so both daily-paid and monthly-paid workers can verify their payslips accurately.

D. Undertime Does Not Offset Overtime (Labor Code Art. 88)

Under Article 88 of the Labor Code, undertime work on any particular day shall not be offset by overtime work on any other day. Permission given to the employee to go on leave on some other day of the week does not exempt the employer from paying the required additional compensation for overtime work rendered.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

How is regular workday overtime pay calculated in the Philippines? ▼
Under Article 87 of the Labor Code, work performed beyond eight (8) hours on an ordinary working day is compensated with an additional 25% of the employee's basic hourly rate (Hourly Rate × 1.25 × Overtime Hours).
What is the difference between a Regular Holiday and a Special (Non-Working) Holiday? ▼
On a Regular Holiday (Art. 94), eligible employees receive 100% of their daily wage even if they do not work, and 200% (double pay) for the first 8 hours if they work. On a Special (Non-Working) Holiday (Art. 93), the "no work, no pay" rule applies if unworked (unless there is a favorable company policy or CBA), and employees who work receive 130% of their basic wage for the first 8 hours (or 150% if it also falls on their scheduled rest day).
How much is the Night Shift Differential (NSD) and what hours are covered? ▼
Under Article 86 of the Labor Code, Night Shift Differential is an additional 10% (minimum) of the employee's applicable hourly wage for each hour worked between 10:00 PM and 6:00 AM. When night shift hours fall on a rest day, holiday, or overtime period, the 10% differential is computed on top of the increased holiday/overtime hourly rate.
Are overtime pay, holiday pay, and night shift differential subject to income tax? ▼
Under Republic Act No. 9504 and the TRAIN Law (RA 10963), Statutory Minimum Wage Earners (MWEs) are 100% exempt from income tax on their basic minimum wage as well as their overtime pay, holiday pay, night shift differential, and hazard pay. For employees earning above the statutory minimum wage, overtime, holiday, and night shift pay form part of gross taxable compensation subject to graduated BIR withholding tax tables.
