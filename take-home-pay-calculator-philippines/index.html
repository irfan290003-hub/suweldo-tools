1. How Take-Home Pay is Computed in the Philippines

In Philippine payroll, your take-home pay (popularly known as your net pay or sahod) is the monetary amount credited to your bank account or distributed on payday. It represents what remains of your contractual gross earnings after subtracting all mandatory statutory contributions and Philippine Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) withholding tax.

Every formal private sector employee registered under Philippine labor statutes must remit monthly contributions to three key social welfare institutions: the Social Security System (SSS), the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth), and the Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG Fund). These statutory contributions are deducted directly from your gross pay before income tax is assessed.

The fundamental mathematical framework used by payroll systems and human resource departments across Metro Manila, Cebu, Davao, and nationwide follows this four-step sequence:

  1. Calculate Gross Compensation: Add your monthly basic salary and any taxable monthly allowances or taxable overtime earnings.
  2. Determine Mandatory Employee Contributions: Compute your employee share for SSS (based on the Monthly Salary Credit schedule), PhilHealth (2.5% of basic salary within the floor and ceiling), and Pag-IBIG Fund (2% of monthly compensation capped at ₱200).
  3. Establish Monthly Taxable Income: Deduct your total mandatory contributions from your gross pay. Because statutory social contributions are legally non-taxable, only the net balance is subject to withholding tax.
  4. Apply the BIR TRAIN Tax Schedule: Annualize your monthly taxable income (multiply by 12), apply the appropriate tax bracket tier under the Republic Act No. 10963 (TRAIN Law), compute the annual liability, and divide by 12 to establish your exact monthly withholding tax deduction.

2. Step-by-Step Worked Example: ₱25,000 Monthly Salary

To understand how these rules operate in practice, let us examine an employee earning a contractual monthly basic salary of ₱25,000.00 with zero additional taxable allowances, working in a standard private corporation and not claiming statutory minimum wage exemption.

Step A: Mandatory Statutory Deductions

  • SSS Deduction (Employee Share): For a monthly salary of ₱25,000.00, the corresponding Monthly Salary Credit (MSC) bracket is exactly ₱25,000.00 (see our complete SSS Contribution Table & MSC Guide). The employee contribution rate is 5% of the MSC (₱1,000 regular SSS + ₱250 WISP provident fund):
    SSS Employee Contribution = ₱25,000.00 × 5% = ₱1,250.00
  • PhilHealth Deduction (Employee Share): Under the 5% total premium schedule, the employee is responsible for half the premium (2.5%), with the employer paying the other 2.5%:
    PhilHealth Contribution = ₱25,000.00 × 2.5% = ₱625.00
  • Pag-IBIG Fund Deduction (Employee Share): The employee rate is 2% of monthly compensation, subject to a statutory maximum fund salary base of ₱10,000.00:
    Pag-IBIG Contribution = ₱10,000.00 (cap) × 2% = ₱200.00
  • Total Mandatory Contributions: ₱1,250.00 + ₱625.00 + ₱200.00 = ₱2,075.00

Step B: Compute Monthly Taxable Income

Taxable income is computed by taking your gross income (₱25,000.00) and subtracting the total mandatory non-taxable contributions (₱2,075.00):

Monthly Taxable Income = ₱25,000.00 − ₱2,075.00 = ₱22,925.00

Step C: Annualize and Calculate BIR Withholding Tax

Next, annualize the monthly taxable amount to identify the applicable TRAIN Law tax tier:

Annual Taxable Income = ₱22,925.00 × 12 months = ₱275,100.00.

According to the annual TRAIN graduated withholding tax table:

  • First ₱250,000.00 is taxed at 0%.
  • Amounts between ₱250,000.00 and ₱400,000.00 are taxed at 15% of the excess over ₱250,000.00.
Excess over ₱250,000.00 = ₱275,100.00 − ₱250,000.00 = ₱25,100.00
Annual Withholding Tax = ₱25,100.00 × 15% = ₱3,765.00
Monthly Withholding Tax = ₱3,765.00 ÷ 12 = ₱313.75

Step D: Calculate Final Net Take-Home Pay

Sum all deductions and subtract them from your gross compensation:

Total Deductions = ₱1,250.00 (SSS) + ₱625.00 (PhilHealth) + ₱200.00 (Pag-IBIG) + ₱313.75 (Tax) = ₱2,388.75
Net Monthly Take-Home Pay = ₱25,000.00 − ₱2,388.75 = ₱22,611.25
Semi-Monthly Cutoff (Sahod bawat kinsenas) = ₱22,611.25 ÷ 2 = ₱11,305.63

3. Overview of Mandatory Deductions in the Philippines

Social Security System (SSS)

The Social Security System provides private sector employees with vital social insurance protection against risks such as sickness, maternity, disability, unemployment, retirement, and death. The total statutory contribution is 15% of the member’s Monthly Salary Credit (MSC), with the employee contributing 5% and the employer subsidizing the remaining 10%. MSC values range from a minimum threshold of ₱5,000 up to an upper ceiling of ₱35,000 in increments of ₱500. Remitting your SSS premiums diligently guarantees eligibility for future pension payments, cash salary loans, and calamity assistance.

Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth)

PhilHealth functions as the national universal health coverage program ensuring accessible medical subsidies, hospitalization discounts, and inpatient care benefits for all Filipino workers. Under the Universal Health Care Act, the premium rate is set at 5% of the worker’s monthly basic salary, split equally between the employee (2.5%) and the employer (2.5%). Contribution calculations are bounded by a mandatory minimum salary floor of ₱10,000 and an upper ceiling of ₱100,000. Maintaining up-to-date PhilHealth contributions ensures hassle-free hospital claim deductions and case-rate package coverage during unforeseen medical emergencies.

Home Development Mutual Fund (Pag-IBIG)

The Pag-IBIG Fund (HDMF) manages national housing micro-finance, affordable residential mortgages, and national provident savings programs for Filipino wage earners. The standard employee contribution rate is 2% of monthly compensation, accompanied by an equal 2% counterpart contribution from the employer. By statutory regulation under HDMF Circular No. 460, the maximum fund salary benchmark is capped at ₱10,000, which fixes the maximum standard employee payroll deduction at ₱200 monthly. In addition to securing low-interest housing loan eligibility, Pag-IBIG savings earn tax-free annual dividends that can be withdrawn upon retirement.

BIR Withholding Income Tax (TRAIN Law)

Withholding tax is an advance tax collected by employers on behalf of the Bureau of Internal Revenue on individual compensation income. Enacted under Republic Act No. 10963 (TRAIN Law), individuals earning an annual taxable income of up to ₱250,000 are completely exempt from personal income tax. For incomes exceeding ₱250,000, tax liability follows a graduated bracket starting at 15% and increasing up to 35% for high earners earning over ₱8,000,000 annually. Statutory non-taxable allowances and mandatory social welfare contributions are deducted prior to tax calculation, significantly reducing your effective tax burden.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Is the 13th month pay taxable? ▼
Under the Tax Reform for Acceleration and Inclusion (TRAIN) Law (Republic Act No. 10963), mandatory 13th month pay and other productivity incentives or Christmas bonuses are tax-exempt up to an aggregate ceiling of ₱90,000.00 per calendar year. Any cumulative bonuses and 13th month payouts received in excess of ₱90,000 are treated as ordinary taxable compensation and subjected to standard graduated withholding tax.
Who pays the employer share of SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG? ▼
By Philippine labor law and statutory agency mandates, your employer is legally obligated to shoulder the designated employer counterpart for SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG Fund out of company operating funds. Employers must remit both your deducted employee share and their own employer contribution to the respective government agencies every month. It is strictly prohibited for employers to pass their required share onto employee payroll.
Are minimum wage earners exempt from income tax? ▼
Yes. Statutory Minimum Wage Earners (SMWE) in both the private and public sectors are 100% exempt from Philippine withholding income tax on their basic minimum wage rate, holiday pay, overtime pay, night shift differential, and hazard pay under Republic Act No. 9504 and the TRAIN Law. However, mandatory social contributions to SSS, PhilHealth, and Pag-IBIG still apply to minimum wage earners.
Why is my actual payslip different from this calculator? ▼
Your actual company payslip may differ due to internal company payroll variables not reflected in baseline statutory formulas. Common causes of variation include: voluntary Pag-IBIG MP2 or SSS WISP Plus savings, company HMO dependent health coverage, tardiness, undertime, or unexcused absence deductions, company emergency loans, union dues, tax-exempt de minimis allowances, or annualized year-end tax adjustments (tax refunds or supplementary tax dues).
How often do contribution rates change? ▼
Contribution adjustments in the Philippines are established by legislative timelines and agency board resolutions. SSS increases are governed by the Social Security Act of 2018 (Republic Act No. 11199), PhilHealth premium rates adjust in accordance with the Universal Health Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223), and Pag-IBIG changes are enacted through circulars issued by the HDMF Board of Trustees. Whenever statutory changes occur, updated contribution tables are released to guide employers and HR practitioners.
What is the difference between gross pay and net pay? ▼
Gross pay is the full compensation stated on your job offer letter or employment contract before any deductions are made—comprising your base salary plus guaranteed monthly allowances, commissions, and overtime pay. Net pay (take-home pay or sahod) is the clean amount transferred to your ATM account after all statutory government contributions (SSS, PhilHealth, Pag-IBIG) and BIR income taxes have been deducted.
