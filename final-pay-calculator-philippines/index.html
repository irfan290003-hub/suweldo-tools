      1. How Final Pay (Back Pay) Is Computed in the Philippines

      Under DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, Series of 2020 (Guidelines on the Payment of Final Pay and Issuance of Certificate of Employment), "Final Pay" or "Back Pay" refers to the sum or totality of all the wages or monetary benefits due the employee regardless of the cause of the termination of employment. Employers are mandated to release final pay within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of separation or termination of employment, unless a more favorable company policy or Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) specifies an earlier release.

      A. Unpaid Earned Salary in Final Cutoff

      Covers all unpaid regular working days rendered up to the employee's last working day, multiplied by the applicable daily basic rate (Monthly Basic Salary × 12 ÷ Annual Divisor), less any verified cutoff tardiness or undertime deductions.

      B. Prorated 13th-Month Pay (PD 851)

      Under Presidential Decree No. 851 and the 1987 Revised DOLE Guidelines, a separated employee who has worked at least one (1) month during the calendar year is entitled to prorated 13th-month pay equal to one-twelfth (1/12) of the total basic salary actually earned within the calendar year, less any prior 13th-month advances already paid.

      C. Cash Conversion of Unused Leaves (Art. 95)

      Under Article 95 of the Labor Code, rank-and-file employees who have rendered at least one (1) year of service are entitled to five (5) days of statutory Service Incentive Leave (SIL), which is commutable to cash if unused. If the employer already provides at least 5 days of paid vacation leave, statutory SIL is deemed complied with (absorbed) unless company policy or CBA explicitly grants both.

      D. Statutory Separation Pay (Arts. 298–299)

      Paid only when employment is terminated for authorized causes under Articles 298 and 299: 1 month pay per year of service for Redundancy or Installation of Labor-Saving Devices, or 1/2 month pay per year of service (minimum 1 month pay) for Retrenchment, Closure not due to serious business losses, or Incurable Disease. For eligible authorized-cause terminations, the statutory minimum is one month's applicable salary basis, even when service is under six months. A fraction of at least six months is considered 1 whole year when calculating the per-year component.

      Salary Basis & Allowance Guidance: The statutory computation uses the entered Monthly Basic Salary (or DOLE Equivalent Monthly Rate) as an informational estimate basis, not a definitive legal determination for every worker. Under Philippine labor law and jurisprudence, allowances are neither automatically included nor automatically excluded; treatment depends on the nature and purpose of the payment, contracts, CBA, and company policy. Allowances already integrated into basic salary must never be counted twice. If allowance classification is uncertain, consult employment records, company HR, or the DOLE, or utilize the optional Custom Separation Pay Override.

      3. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

      When should final pay (back pay) be released in the Philippines? ▼
      Under DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06, Series of 2020, an employer must release an employee's final pay within thirty (30) calendar days from the date of separation or termination of employment, unless a more favorable company policy, individual contract, or Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) specifies an earlier release period. Alongside final pay, the employer must issue a Certificate of Employment (COE) within three (3) days from the time of the employee's request.
      Do employees who voluntarily resign get separation pay? ▼
      Generally, no. Under Article 300 [formerly 285] of the Labor Code, an employee who voluntarily resigns is not entitled to statutory separation pay, unless separation pay upon resignation is expressly granted in the employment contract, company policy, established company practice, or Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA). However, resigned employees still receive their unpaid earned salary, prorated 13th-month pay, and cash conversion of unused convertible leave credits.
      How is statutory Service Incentive Leave (SIL) converted to cash in final pay? ▼
      Under Article 95 of the Labor Code and the DOLE Handbook, rank-and-file employees who have rendered at least one (1) year of service are entitled to a yearly Service Incentive Leave of five (5) days with pay. Any unused SIL at the time of separation is commutable to its money equivalent based on the employee's daily basic rate at termination. Managerial employees and employees already enjoying at least 5 days of paid vacation leave from their employer are exempt from statutory SIL.
      Is separation pay subject to BIR income tax or withholding tax? ▼
      Under Section 32(B)(6)(b) of the National Internal Revenue Code (NIRC), separation pay received by an employee due to death, sickness, other physical disability, or any cause beyond the control of the employee—such as redundancy, installation of labor-saving devices, retrenchment to prevent losses, or business closure—is 100% exempt from income tax and withholding tax. Conversely, voluntary separation or contractual ex-gratia payouts upon ordinary resignation are generally taxable unless covered by a BIR-qualified retirement benefit plan.
      How are unused vacation leave and sick leave taxed when monetized? ▼
      Under BIR Revenue Regulations No. 29-2025 (effective January 6, 2026, further amending RR No. 2-98, as amended), the monetized value of unused vacation leave credits of private-sector employees not exceeding twelve (12) days during the calendar year is considered a tax-exempt de minimis benefit. In this calculator, statutory Service Incentive Leave (SIL) and company vacation leave share one combined 12-day de minimis ceiling under a conservative tax assumption (as no explicit BIR issuance states that SIL enjoys an independent separate exemption). Any monetized vacation leave exceeding 12 combined days, along with any monetized private-sector sick leave credits, is added to the employee's 13th-month pay and other benefits pool, which is exempt from income tax up to the aggregate ₱90,000 ceiling under Section 32(B)(7)(e) of the Tax Code.
      Are regular or fixed allowances included in the salary basis for statutory separation pay? ▼

      No blanket rule applies: Under Philippine labor standards and Supreme Court jurisprudence, allowances are neither automatically included in nor automatically excluded from the statutory separation-pay salary base.

      • Nature and Purpose of the Allowance: Where an allowance is regular, customary, unconditional, and forms an integral part of the employee's standard compensation for services rendered (such as integrated cost-of-living allowances or fixed pay packaged as allowances), jurisprudence may count it toward "basic wage" or "salary." Conversely, contingent payments, variable performance stipends, discretionary bonuses, and actual expense reimbursements (e.g., travel, gasoline, meal subsidies, representation expenses) are generally excluded.
      • Contracts, CBA, and Company Policy: The definition of "salary" or "severance base" established in your employment contract, employee handbook, or Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) is often decisive.
      • No Double Counting: Any allowance that is already incorporated into your stated Monthly Basic Salary must not be added a second time.
      • Estimate Basis vs. Legal Determination: The Monthly Basic Salary field in this calculator provides an estimate basis for standard payroll projections; it is not a binding legal determination of an individual employee's statutory entitlement.
      • Custom Separation Pay Override: The calculator offers an optional "Custom / Contractual Separation Pay Override" as a manual estimation input if your employer, contract, or CBA provides an agreed severance amount, but this override is an optional input, not an automatic legal entitlement.
      • When Classification is Uncertain: Employees and employers with questions about specific allowances should review their employment contracts, company policies, or collective agreements, or seek qualified guidance from the DOLE or labor counsel.
      Can an employer automatically deduct unreturned company laptops or unliquidated claims from final pay? ▼
      Under Articles 113 and 116 of the Labor Code, employers cannot unilaterally deduct disputed property damages or unliquidated accountabilities from an employee's statutory wages without the employee's written authorization or due process, although the Supreme Court (Milan v. NLRC, G.R. No. 202961) recognizes that employers may require standard clearance procedures for the return of company property before releasing clearance-dependent items.

      Important Statutory & Payroll Disclaimers

      • Informational Estimate Only: This calculator provides estimates based on statutory Philippine labor standards (DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06-20, Labor Code of the Philippines, PD 851, and BIR TRAIN Law rules). It does not constitute formal legal, accounting, or payroll advice.
      • Company Policy & CBA Variations: Actual employer computations may vary depending on your company's specific annual divisor convention, contractual leave conversion rules, or more generous Collective Bargaining Agreement (CBA) terms.
      • Separation Pay Salary Base & Allowances: Monthly Basic Salary serves as an estimate baseline. Allowances are neither universally included nor universally excluded in statutory separation pay under Philippine jurisprudence; treatment depends on the nature of the payment, contract terms, and company policy, and integrated allowances must not be double-counted.
      • BIR Form 2316 Year-End Tax Annualization: Final withholding tax adjustments (tax refund or withholding tax deficit) depend on your cumulative year-to-date gross compensation, mandatory contributions, and tax already withheld by your employer on BIR Form 2316. Any withholding tax deficit is shown separately as a payroll tax reconciliation amount and is not automatically deducted from statutory net final pay.
      • 30-Day Release Guideline: DOLE Labor Advisory No. 06-20 prescribes a 30-calendar-day release timeline from the date of separation, subject to institution-specific clearance procedures for company property return.
