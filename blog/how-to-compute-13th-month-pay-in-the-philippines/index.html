How to Compute 13th Month Pay in the Philippines

13th month pay is one-twelfth of the basic salary you earned in the year. Here is who gets it, how to compute it, when it is paid, and when it is taxed.

13th month pay is a yearly benefit that private-sector rank-and-file employees receive from their employer. The minimum amount is one-twelfth of the basic salary you earned during the calendar year. Employers must pay it on or before December 24.

13th month pay = Total basic salary earned in the calendar year ÷ 12

Below, I explain who gets it, what counts as basic salary, and how the tax rules work, with four worked examples. You can also try your own numbers in the 13th Month Pay Calculator. The results are estimates.

What Is 13th Month Pay?

13th month pay is required by Presidential Decree No. 851, which was signed in 1975. It is paid on top of your regular salary. Many workers get it in December, and many people also call it a Christmas bonus. The two are not the same thing. 13th month pay is a legal minimum. A Christmas bonus is extra money that an employer gives by choice.

Who Gets 13th Month Pay

All rank-and-file employees in the private sector get 13th month pay, as long as they worked at least one month during the calendar year. This is true whatever your job title, employment status or way of being paid. Regular, probationary and casual employees are all covered.

Some people are not covered. Managerial employees are not entitled to it under the decree. Some employers are also exempt, for example government offices and employers that already pay an equivalent benefit. If you are not sure about your case, ask your HR department or DOLE.

When 13th Month Pay Is Paid

The deadline is December 24 each year. An employer may pay it in two parts: half before the school year opens, and the rest on or before December 24. Employees who resign or are separated before the payment date still get a pro-rated amount for the months they worked.

What Counts as Basic Salary

The formula uses your basic salary, not your total pay. The Revised Guidelines on the 13th month pay law define basic salary as the earnings you receive for the work you do.

Included in basic salary

  • Your regular monthly, daily or hourly pay for work done
  • Pay for the days you actually worked during the year
  • Salary changes during the year, counted month by month

Not included in basic salary

  • Overtime pay and other premium pay
  • Night shift differential
  • Holiday pay
  • The cash value of unused vacation and sick leave
  • Allowances and benefits that are not part of your regular basic salary

There is one exception. If your company contract, agreement or long-standing practice treats any of these items as part of your basic salary, then they count. Check your employment contract.

How to Compute 13th Month Pay, Step by Step

Step 1: Add up your basic salary for the calendar year

Add what you earned as basic salary from January to December. If you were paid the same amount every month, multiply it by the number of months you worked. If your salary changed during the year, add each period separately.

Step 2: Take out unpaid days

If you had unpaid leave or unpaid absences, your basic salary earned is lower, so subtract those amounts.

Step 3: Divide by 12

Divide the total basic salary you earned by 12. The result is your 13th month pay.

Step 4: Check the tax limit

Add your 13th month pay to any other benefits you received, such as a Christmas bonus. Compare the total with the ₱90,000 tax-exempt limit. The next section explains how this works.

Look at the third example. If you used your current salary (₱25,000) × 12, you would get ₱25,000. The correct amount is ₱22,500, because you earned ₱20,000 a month for the first six months.

Is 13th Month Pay Taxable?

Mostly no. Under the TRAIN Law, the 13th month pay and other benefits are exempt from income tax up to a combined limit of ₱90,000 a year. Only the amount above ₱90,000 is taxable.

Here is an example. Say you get ₱25,000 in 13th month pay and ₱70,000 in other benefits, such as bonuses.

The ₱5,000 is not taxed as a flat fee. It is added to your taxable income for the year, and your employer works out the final tax at year end. You can see how monthly tax is estimated in our guide to computing take-home pay.

Common Mistakes

  • Multiplying your current salary by 12. The formula uses what you actually earned, so a raise or a late start changes the result.
  • Adding overtime, night differential or holiday pay. These are not part of basic salary, unless your company treats them that way.
  • Forgetting unpaid leave. Unpaid days lower the total basic salary you earned.
  • Mixing up 13th month pay and a Christmas bonus. Only the 13th month pay is required by law.
  • Ignoring the ₱90,000 limit. Add your bonuses to the 13th month pay before you check for tax.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Who is entitled to 13th month pay? ▾

All rank-and-file employees in the private sector who worked at least one month during the calendar year, regardless of job title, employment status or how they are paid. Managerial employees are not covered.

When must employers pay 13th month pay? ▾

On or before December 24 each year. An employer may pay half before the school year opens and the rest by December 24.

Is 13th month pay taxable? ▾

Mostly no. Under the TRAIN Law, 13th month pay and other benefits are exempt from income tax up to a combined limit of ₱90,000 a year. Only the amount above that limit is taxable.

Do I get 13th month pay if I resign before December? ▾

Yes, you get a pro-rated amount for the months you worked, as long as you worked at least one month during the year.

Does 13th month pay include overtime and allowances? ▾

No. The formula uses basic salary only. Overtime, night shift differential, holiday pay and allowances that are not part of your regular basic salary are not counted, unless your company contract or practice treats them as part of it.

How do I compute 13th month pay if my salary changed during the year? ▾

Add the basic salary you earned in each period, then divide the total by 12. Do not multiply your current salary by 12.
